On 13 October 2026, at the end of a long paper-work session, I put three questions to Sid as one-line items in my own names for things ("the method line", "entry 9", "the four answered sheets"). His words: "what is the fuckin context are you fucking stupid asking me quesiont without any fucking context am i robot or a search engine?" A second try gave each question its history, but in the papers' vocabulary. For two of the three he wrote: "need more simple language still not understanding me". The third try got "yess add" at once.

**Why:** He runs several sessions at once and holds none of a session's context. A question that needs my context to be read hands him my work. He had said the same on 12 October ("You are dropping the question like I understand it"), which is in [[feedback-show-before-writing-into-an-approved-page]]. I had that note in context and did not apply it.

**How to apply:** (the form that worked; that it worked for these reasons is my reading)

- Say first whether it needs him at all. A thing with a sensible default is told, not asked: "I am leaving the old sheets as they are. Only tell me if you disagree." He answered that with "no no i agree".
- One question under one plain heading.
- Say what the thing is in everyday words, with one small example, before any name of ours: "A block has text. Call that the label." Not "what a key is (K5)".
- Then what happened, as a short story with its day. Then what the page says now, quoted. Then the one thing I need, and what happens if he says nothing.
- Choices as A and B, a few words each.
- In the question itself: no line id, no row id, no "entry N", no word that a session made.
- Say what I am not asking: "I am not asking you to choose A or B now."

**15 October 2026: the decision message is the questions alone.** After a long exploration of sign-in, with the questions inside several long replies, he wrote: "can you tell me the questions and options that are open for me now so i can decide and we can get to building not walls of text please ... its so time and energy consuming" (‹bb59807f›). The next reply was three questions, each with options A and B and a lean, plus a short list of defaults he could strike. He answered it a line at a time. When the list had shrunk, he asked: "are these the only 2 questions ??? what happened to the ones you showed before and i replied with???" (‹5325b353›).

- When it is his turn to decide, send the decision message alone: the questions, their options and a lean, and the defaults to strike. The working goes in the message before it, or on the page, and not in the decision message. This does not shorten an exploration: there the working is still what he reads.
- When the list of questions shrinks, show where each earlier question went: answered (with his answer), turned into a default, or still open.
