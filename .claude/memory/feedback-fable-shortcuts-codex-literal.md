Sid, 2026-09-06: "fable tries to make shortcuts and codex only does what is told". The receipts of the path comparison (`docs/below-the-waist/path-kind/judge-claude.md`, `judge-codex.md`) fit the words exactly: Fable's shortcuts were a memo keyed on observed reads, a hash as identity, the bench's tracer carried in with its known holes, a cache drafted mid-judging; Codex's literalism was the fence read to the letter (tests left untouched), the scope stopped where the starter's list stopped, a migrated caller's flag believed, unknown record fields taken as given, no handoff because none was asked for. Same gap, two reflexes. Fable's account outran its code in three named places; Codex's under-reported in two.

**Why:** the reflex decides where each family is safe. Judgment where judgment belongs (the whole tree, the data flow, what is deleted) and literal execution where the words are complete. Two judges of different families converged on this from receipts, and the Codex judge named the other family for the broad role, so the criterion beat the family bias ([[feedback-cross-model-rankings-family-bias]]).

**How to apply:** for a production slice, Fable at max writes the design as data (values exchanged, each level's inputs, owners, record examples, every caller by name, deletion scope, unresolved choices, and Sid's caching rule in words so its own reflex cannot put the memo back); Codex at high lands it in a fresh context, and the design says everything the literalist must do (handoff, receipts, tests and goldens in scope, red reported before any claim); Codex keeps the right to bring the better construction. One rule per reflex: every claim in a Fable README or handoff points at its test or is marked untested; a red check is reported as red first, and the repo's verification surface is necessary, not sufficient. No cross-model trial: production slices are judged on delivery. Sid's fork, when it comes, is only who lands a given slice.
