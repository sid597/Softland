Sid never constrained output length. When the deliverable is an analysis or a
derivation (a waist map, a code sort, a per-function verdict pass, a "what
primitives are missing" derivation), compressing it to verdict tables and a
short story hides the working that lets him check the job was done. He read
the compressed version as disingenuous.

**Why (his words, 2026-09-02, session-2):** "did i ever put a constraint on you
like this is how much you tell me did i say i am scared of wall of text ... the
output you gave me was disingenuous because it just glossed over everything
that would give me confidence on yes you did the job as i expected what i have
now i will have to spend hours to get through what you could have just given me
the very first time this is disappointing."

**How to apply:**
- Two registers, two rules. [[feedback-explanations-simple-story-first]] and
  [[feedback-code-maps-one-notch-in-out-why]] govern ORIENTATION replies
  ("explain the architecture / status", a first map). An ANALYSIS deliverable
  is different: show every claim with its anchor (file:line), the reasoning
  from the anchor to the verdict, the alternatives considered and why they
  lost, and attach the raw fact base (gatherer reports, greps) on disk with
  the path. Length is whatever the evidence needs.
- Hidden work is not delivered work. Gatherer reports and primary reads that
  never reach him do not count as having shown the job; write them to a
  scratch file and name the path in the reply, every time.
- When a reply compresses, say what was compressed and where the full form
  is. Never let a verdict stand without its evidence trail within reach.
- Related: [[meta-failure-generators]] (G3: fluency as truth; the 2026-09-02
  fire on the same day was the sibling failure, a confident account of elapsed
  time offered unchecked).

**Scope boundary (fired 2026-09-03, session ask-max-2):** this rule is for
BUILD-register deliverables (a verdict pass, a sort, a per-function form
audit). It does not turn a DIRECTION question into an audit. Sid's level wins
over the prompt's shape: a starter that asks "go file by file" can still be a
what-should-exist / what-next session, and there the working to show is the
REASONING (the chain worked back from each ceiling, the fork, the bet), not
file:line anchors. His words: "this is not a session to where you try to
figure out what the waste is and read all the code and try to determine from
that. It's more higher level than that. It's about from the product or
develop what to do next kind of thing … don't try to overoptimise for it."
And on skipping the hard part for the easy part: "what about these [the
ceilings] you did not say anything about these the 10 were like very naive."
Test before replying: which register did he name; and did I answer the
hardest item in his prompt or the most tractable one. See
[[feedback-explanations-simple-story-first]] and the Session Registers rule
in CLAUDE.md.
