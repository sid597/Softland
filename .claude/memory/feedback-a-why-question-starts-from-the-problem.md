On 13 October 2026 Sid asked why the store has a log of answers and why a gate reads a clock. I answered both from the record: "On 8 October you chose that a stamp holds clock-time+counter", and a ten-step list of who said what on which day. He was still confused after three answers. His words, dictated:

"when I ask you about why is something something a problem, don't start with what I said. Start with what is the problem that we are tackling. And why is it a problem? It's a fresh start for you to look into the problem from a fresh perspective and decide if it is a problem to be brought up and what are our thoughts on it. Not like just because something was decided and it makes a problem. Based on that, it has to be a real problem. It is not a based on what was done."

**Why:** A list of decisions says how a thing came to be. It does not say whether the thing is needed. Answering from the record also protects what was built: a part that exists only to work around an earlier choice then reads as if it solved a real problem. In this case the fresh look found that two parts of the store (the clock of a batch, and half of the case for the log of answers) exist because the gate reads a clock at the moment it decides, and that nobody had weighed reading it earlier. The record could not show that; only the problem could.

**How to apply:** (the order is my reading of his words)

- Say the problem in everyday words first: what goes wrong, for whom, and when. No date, no name of a sheet, no "you decided".
- Test whether it is real: where the failure can actually be seen, how often, what it costs. Say plainly when it is not real, or is narrow, or exists only because of an earlier choice.
- Then lay out the ways to solve it from nothing, with the ones that nobody built. Say whether the built one is the only one. Take a position.
- The record comes after, and only where it changes the judgment or he asks for it. It is evidence about how we got here, never the reason a thing is needed.

It goes with [[feedback-a-question-to-sid-carries-its-own-context]]: both ask that he is handed the thing itself, not my path to it.
