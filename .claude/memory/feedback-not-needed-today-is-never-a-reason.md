On 13 October 2026 Sid asked why the store's shared space is spread over several partitions when Rama's rule is that what must stay in order goes into one partition. I answered: "There is no need for it today", "that load does not exist", "keep the shared space in one partition until a measured load says otherwise". Over the hour before I had also marked parts as "no present need" and "nothing built reads it yet". His words:

"What is this … logic that it is not a problem today? I'm not making a coffee, I'm making a system that will live forever."

**Why:** He is building a foundation, not a first version. A shared space that many people write and read will outgrow one thread; that is a fact of the system he is building, whether or not a single record exists today. An argument from today's load answers a question he is not asking and throws away the reason the part was designed. The project page already names this failure: "you are too much into what exists today, and you cannot see what should exist and what will exist". I had it in context and still argued from the empty store.

**How to apply:** (my reading)

- Judge a part against the system at full life: many writers, many readers, years of data, rules that change. Ask whether the part is right for that system.
- "Nothing uses it yet", "no load yet", "not measured yet" are facts about the present. Say them as facts when they matter (what is proven, what is run). Never offer them as a reason to remove, shrink or delay a part.
- When a part looks heavy, the question is whether it is the right long-lived answer to a real problem, and what the other long-lived answers are. Not whether the problem has arrived.
- A cheaper form for now is his call, and only when he asks for it.

It sharpens [[feedback-a-why-question-starts-from-the-problem]]: testing whether a problem is real means real for the system that is being built, not for the store as it stands this week.
