Sid, 23 September 2026, ruling on whether an act lands one fact or several:
"we are providing a spectrum and if there are two outcomes on the spectrum on
either end then we are providing the whole spectrum. it's not an either-or.
the choosing remains with the user and the toolmaker and everyone else but
not us. we can provide some good defaults."

**Why:** The store is the fixed side; what a tool makes a fact of, and what a
group does along a dependency chain, are live. Deciding those in the store
would be the store choosing for people.

**How to apply:** When a question reads "should the store do X or Y" and both
X and Y are things a tool or a group could want, the answer is "the store
supports both, with a default"; do not ask Sid to pick. The boundary is
uniformity: a rule every record follows (encoding, envelope growth rule,
naming scheme, act id on every fact) is one rule, never a spectrum, and must
be fixed before the first record. Test from the same session: could two
tools follow different rules here without every reader carrying both for
ever? If not, it is the store's to fix. See [[first-record-dimensions]] when
written.

**How the default is picked.** Sid, 12 October 2026, asked what the store
keeps of a person's reads when nobody has chosen: "by default if its not set
then we take the side of spectrum that captures more because we are not
choosing the destructive side by defualt that can happen even after but the
other side cannot." He gave it against a session's suggestion of the other
end. On 11 and 12 October he chose the same way three more times: the log is
never trimmed, a shared log is never cut for a forget, and a value is plain
in the log unless someone chooses a lock for it.

**Why:** A thing that was not kept cannot be kept afterwards. A thing that
was kept can still be dropped.

**How to apply:** When a default between two ends is open, and one end keeps
a thing while the other drops it or makes it droppable, suggest the end that
keeps. That it reaches every such default is my reading; he said it for
reads. "Simple by default" is not the rule: I read his "lets go with simple
policy answer by default" that way, suggested the dropping end, and he
corrected it.
