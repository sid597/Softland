2026-07-15, first-light step proposals: I wrote "blank ground, a cursor" and "answer comes out as blocks." Sid: cursor WHERE — top-left, center, three-quarters — what is the assumption and why? Why assume the output shape at all? If blocks, arranged how, why? "These are the type of things you guys miss ... living from the user's pov, building the world model for each step, then question why, what next, will the user know the next step, is the flow liveable ... put some effort before bringing anything to me."

**Why:** every unexamined word ("cursor", "message", "panel", "bar", "bubble") smuggles an app-world default with no provenance — the discarded old UI was built entirely of such defaults. Sid evaluates by living the flow in his head (feelings first); a proposal that cannot answer "why" at each visible detail reads as zero effort regardless of how much architecture sits under it.

**How to apply:** before bringing any design or step to Sid, walk it moment by moment from his point of view. For each moment answer four things: what exactly do you SEE (position, shape, grain) · what can you DO · how do you KNOW · WHY — where every why is (a) derived from a stated principle, (b) cited to Sid's own words (quote the LOG/voice note), or (c) explicitly labeled a taste-call with options and a default-with-reason. Then test the flow forward: does each why get answered, does the user know the next step. Treat common UI nouns as assumption alarms and interrogate each one.
