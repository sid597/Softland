Why every new idea spread

You say the cause is that Rama and Softland were never kept apart. That is true. I think it is one case of three deeper causes. 02-box-and-levels.md § 2

a. The architecture was drawn as a pipeline.

  • A pipeline has no levels. Whatever travels with the data passes through every row: who, identity, time, permission. So every decision about one of those touches every row.
  • If you split a program into modules by processing step, every change spreads. If each module hides one decision that is likely to change, changes stay local.

b. One word, three meanings.

  • When one word spans levels, a thought about one level slides into the others.

What a level is

  • Words: the few nouns and verbs you think in at that level.
  • Promises: what it guarantees to the level above.
  • A secret: what it hides, so it can change without anyone above noticing.
  • One direction: it uses only the words and promises of the levels below it.

So last turn's definition of a level grows by one part: words, promises, secrets, one direction, and knobs. 03-breakouts-A-to-F.md § F

Four tests that a boundary is real

  1. The reasons to change differ. New storage technology changes the fact level. A new social model changes the permission level.
  2. The swap test. Could you replace what is below without anyone above noticing?
  3. The think test. Can you work at the level using only its own words?
  4. Pace. Does the level change at its own speed?

A level's promises are exactly what a proof is about, and that proof may assume only the promises of the levels below. An agent is not a level; it is an inhabitant that uses the interfaces. 02-box-and-levels.md § 7

Do not design every level up front.
