## More words that the questions use

- **the base** — the layer that the whole store shares. The system is at the top of its chain of permissions (decision 1 of 8 October).
- **streaming, microbatch** — the two classes of layer ("class" is a borrowed word). A streaming layer's acts are decided one at a time, in milliseconds. A microbatch layer's acts are decided in batches, a moment later. The base is microbatch; every other layer starts streaming (decision 4 of 7 October).
- **permission** — a fact in a layer that lets its holder act there. It says who holds it, in which layer, where it was given, and which permission is above it.
- **store key** — a fact key that the store itself acts on.
- **forget** — the store destroys a lock; what it sealed can never be unsealed.
- **freezes** — a thing freezes on the day the first record is kept for good: after that day a change of it splits the store into before and after. Until then a change of it costs a change of code.
- **a session suggests** — what the AI session that wrote this sheet would pick, with its reason. It is not a default.
- **the rulings file** — the rulings of 23–25 September, as an AI session wrote them down.
- **the ledger** — a September paper in which a session wrote down what you said.

=== W1 | Who may see a layer

Kind: **system sets** — one form, for every layer. **Freezes.**

```
 To act in a layer, an act names a permission (decision 8 of 6 October).
 For seeing, two lines exist:
   your words of 8 October      a user who is not signed in is "just roaming around on what is open
                                by the system or world group"
   the rulings file, 24 Sept    "Default visibility: base open to any authenticated actor; a
                                person's own and session layers private to that person; group
                                layers visible to the group's members. Seed policy facts, so a
                                store can differ."
 Both say WHO sees. The rulings file adds that this is kept as facts, which a store can change. No
 line says which facts. That is this question.
 Your words of 8 October on a permission: it ties back to who "gave the permission to do xyz in
 their group". A permission of today has no slot for "xyz".
```

**How does a layer say who may see it?**

[ ] **A** — it does not. Whoever holds a permission in a layer may see it. Nobody else.
If A: one rule, and nothing more is kept. A person who holds no permission in a layer sees nothing of it. To let someone see is to let them write.
[ ] **B** — by a setting on the layer: "open to everyone", "open to everyone signed in", or "closed". In a closed layer, whoever holds a permission may see it.
If B: a layer can be seen by people who hold no permission in it. The setting is one more store key. In a closed layer, to let someone see is to let them write.
[ ] **C** — by that setting, and by permissions that say what they allow: see, or see and act.
If C: a guest can see and not write, and a holder can give "see only" beneath their own. The setting is one more store key, and a permission gets one slot more than the form of 6 October has.

Today: nothing checks who sees.

A session suggests: **C**. Your line on a person who is not signed in needs a thing that is "open" to someone who holds no permission: the setting. Your line on a permission says "to do xyz": a slot for what it allows.

=== W3 | Where the three steps of a read are done

Kind: **system sets**. Can change later.

```
 On the way in, the gate is the authority, and not the door (F8 of 7 October). The reason written
 with that decision, in a session's words: "anything can append to a depot without the door".
 The way out has the same two places for its three steps (see, find, unseal):
   in the store       only the store's own code reads the record. Rama can keep a table from every
                      program outside the store.
   in the runtime     the runtime reads the tables directly, and gives each asker only what that
                      asker may see.
 Neither place can check WHO asks. Sign-in tells the runtime, and the runtime tells the store. So a
 fault in the runtime that asks in the wrong person's name gets that person's things in both.
```

**Where are the three steps of a read done?**

[ ] **A** — in the store. No program outside the store can read the record's tables.
If A: a program that reaches Rama cannot read a table directly, and the check is written once, in the store. The store unseals, and plain values go from the store to the runtime. Rama pushes a change by itself only from a table that outside programs can read: see W4.
[ ] **B** — in the runtime. The runtime reads the record's tables directly.
If B: less to build, and Rama pushes each change to the runtime with its value. The runtime holds locks and unseals. Each program that reaches Rama can read every table, so the check is only as good as the runtime's code.

Today: programs outside the store can read the tables. The three reads that are built (an act's answer, a layer's class, a lock) are the store's own code.

A session suggests: **A**. One place for the check, the same place as on the way in. Its price is in W4.
