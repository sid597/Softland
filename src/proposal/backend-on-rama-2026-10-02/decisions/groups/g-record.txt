## The words that the questions share

- **who** — the one who makes an act: a person or an agent. **person** — someone who signs in. **agent** — a program, often a model, that makes acts for a person or a group. **tool** — a program run by a person or an agent; its acts can carry a permission of its own, beneath theirs. **group** — people who share a layer of their own.
- **layer** — the one place in the store that an act writes to: the base that all share, a group's, a person's, or a session's.
- **session** — the sitting of work that an act belongs to; a person's starts at a sign-in. Every act names its session (decision 4 of 6 October).
- **permission** — a fact in a layer that lets its **holder** write there: [holder · layer · granted-in], with a fourth slot for the permission above it, if there is one. **granted-in** — the layer where it was written. act.clj calls the holder's slot "who". Every act names one (decision 8 of 6 October).
- **chain** — a permission, the one above it, and so on to the top. **root** — a permission with nothing above it. **the top of a chain** — its root, or a session's permission if nothing stands above it; it is held for a person, a group or the system. **revoke** — a fact that ends a permission. **policy** — the facts of a layer that say who may see it and who may write in it.
- **subjects** — the people that an act is about.
- **forget** — the store destroys a lock: what it sealed can never be opened again, and a read of the past shows "erased on" the date.
- **the system** — the store acting for itself, for no person: its first facts, the base, its own work. **operator** — the person who runs this store.
- **frozen at the first kept record** — after the first act that the store keeps for good, a choice cannot change without splitting the store into acts before and acts after.
- **the rulings file** — PROGRESS.md "Now" in src/proposal/frame-2026-09-15: the rulings of 23–25 September, as a session wrote them down. **the 2 October drawing** — ARCHITECTURE-STE100.md.

Kind: **system sets** — one choice for the whole store.

- **link** — one fact, kept apart from the record, that joins an id to the person it stands for.

```
 Alice signs in with Google. The 2 October drawing says that Google tells the store who she is, and that
 at her first sign-in the store makes a person for her, and her own layer. Then her id stands in
   each act she makes                  :who <her id>
   each permission she holds           [<her id> :alice-notes :alice-notes]
   each act about her                  :subjects #{<her id>}
 two ids for Alice                     A  #uuid "7f3c…"   says nothing; one link joins it to her sign-in
                                       B  :alice          readable; made from her sign-in
 The rulings file lists "opaque actor ids with one erasable link to the person" as open.
```

**Which id stands for a person in the record?**

[ ] **A** — an id that says nothing (a UUID), joined to the person by one link that the store can erase.
If A: the record names nobody by itself, and a reader needs the link to see that it was Alice; to forget her, the store erases the one link.
[ ] **B** — a readable id, made from the sign-in.
If B: the record names Alice wherever she acted; to forget her, her id is taken out of each act and permission, by an update of the store's code.

=== E3 · H7 | Is there a root above a session?

Kind: **system sets** — one choice for the whole store.

```
 Alice signs in; the store starts her session :s12. In it she writes in her own layer :alice-notes.
 The rulings file, as ruled in chat on 23–24 September: "The session is the root; narrower permissions
 are ordinary facts under it." And on 24–25 September: "a permission lives in the layer it governs; a gate
 checks only permissions in layers it orders". The proposal R7 of 25 September reads that first line for
 each layer, with a root above the session: B.

 A  the session's permission is the top     in :alice-notes, :s12's permission has nothing above it
                                            chain: session ─ agent ─ tool
 B  a root above the session                :alice-notes is made with a root; opening :s12 writes
                                            :s12's permission beneath it, through that layer's gate
                                            chain: root ─ session ─ agent ─ tool
```

**In a layer, is a session's permission the top of its chain, or is there a root above it?**

[ ] **A** — the top: nothing stands above a session's permission.
If A: what a session may write in a layer is set when the session starts, from the layer's policy; to end it, the session's permission is revoked.
[ ] **B** — a root above it, made with the layer.
If B: a session's permission is written beneath the layer's root, through that layer's gate; to end it, it is revoked, or one above it.

Today: act.clj: a permission has a slot for the one above it, 4 deep at most (decision 8; the depth is kept from today's code, not decided). Nothing in the store's tree writes a permission yet.

=== E2 | Who may write a permission beneath another

Kind: **group chooses — the default is the question.** Who may write what in a layer is that layer's policy; a group can change it for its layer.

- **narrower** — a permission beneath another lets its holder do no more than the one above it lets.

```
 Alice, in her session :s12, starts an agent to sort her notes in :alice-notes.
 The agent's acts need a permission of their own: narrower, beneath :s12's.
 The rulings file, 23–24 September: "narrower permissions are ordinary facts under it".
 Who may write that fact?
```

**By default, who may write a narrower permission beneath one in a layer?**

[ ] **A** — whoever holds the permission above it.
If A: Alice gives her agent a part of what she may do, and asks no one; a member of a group does the same for her own agents.
[ ] **B** — only whoever stands at the top of the chain (E1 · H8).
If B: in her own layer Alice writes it herself; in a group's layer, whoever stands at the top of the group's chain writes it.
[ ] **C** — only the operator.
If C: every narrower permission is written by the operator, and no layer can change that; it becomes a rule that the system sets.

Today: nothing in the store's tree writes or checks a permission; act.clj checks only that a permission's layer is the act's layer (decision 8).

=== E5 | After a session closes

Kind: **group chooses — the default is the question.** A layer, or a tool, can change it for its own acts.

- **close** — the end of a session: a person signs out, or an agent's run ends.

```
 14:00  Alice's session :s12 starts. She asks an agent for a summary of her notes.
 14:20  Alice signs out: :s12 closes.
 14:25  The agent's reply comes back, as an act in :s12, under a permission beneath :s12's.
```

**By default, does a permission still stand after its session closes?**

[ ] **A** — no: when a session closes, the permissions beneath it end.
If A: the 14:25 reply is refused, and the refusal is recorded; work that must go on after its session needs a layer or a tool that says so.
[ ] **B** — yes: a permission stands until it is revoked, whether its session is open or closed.
If B: the 14:25 reply lands after Alice has gone; to end what a session may do takes a revoke of its own.

=== H2 | What a stamp holds

- **wall time** — the time of day by a clock, in milliseconds.
- **counter** — a number that goes up by one.

```
 A stamp is one integer (decision 12 of 6 October). The rulings file, ruling 4 (24 September): "Never
 backward within a unit; never earlier than anything the fact stood on."

 the integer holds                what a person can ask of the stamps alone
 the wall time and a counter      "the notes as they were at 15:00 yesterday"
 a counter alone                  "which came first?" — not "at what time?"
```

**Does a stamp hold the wall time?**

[ ] **A** — yes: the wall time and a counter, in one integer.
If A: a read "as of 15:00 yesterday" is one number in every layer; a stamp stays close to the clock.
[ ] **B** — no: a counter alone, which only goes up.
If B: stamps give the order of acts only; the time of an act by a clock is a fact of its own, or the maker's own claim.

=== H1 | Forgetting one value: the depot's copy

Kind: **system sets** — one choice for the whole store.

- **the depot's copy** — the act as the door appended it, values sealed; the depot keeps it until a trim.

```
 10:00  In session :s12 Alice writes note-41. The door seals it with a lock and appends it to the
        streaming depot; the gate accepts it into the record.
 10:05  Alice forgets note-41.
 The rulings file, ruling 7 (24 September): "every value gets its own small lock at write"; a setting on
 the layer can make it one lock for each act.

 A  a lock for each value, from the door on    10:05: note-41's lock is destroyed; no copy of it can be
                                               opened, in the record or in the depot
 B  one lock for each session and layer        10:05: note-41's copy in the depot still opens with :s12's
                                               lock, until that lock is destroyed or the depot is trimmed
```

**When one value is forgotten, is the depot's copy of it gone at once?**

[ ] **A** — yes: the door seals each value with its own lock (one for each act, where the layer sets so).
If A: the door needs a lock for each value that it seals; forgetting one note reaches every copy of it at once.
[ ] **B** — no: the door seals what a session sends to a layer with one lock.
If B: the door needs one lock for each session and layer; a forgotten note opens in the depot until that lock goes, and when it goes, so does all else that the session sent there.

Today: no code of the store's tree makes, keeps or destroys a lock yet; decision 3 of 6 October puts the seal in the door. Finding: the log keeps every appended value until a trim. Frozen at the first kept record.
