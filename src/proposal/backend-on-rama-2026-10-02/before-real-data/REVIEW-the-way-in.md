## Checked and found right

The type `Door` has six fields, none that a call changes. Proof: the record in the log is the maker's act with the two parts; `1.50M` and `2N` are in the log as given and the row holds `{:a [1 2] :b 1.5M}`, written out in the proof; a list goes as a list; a second door reads the first door's answer.
- **A4, R4, entry 7**, "The door checks nothing of an act before it appends it": `additions/reading` reads `:layer` alone (run: a thing whose who, session, permission and facts throw gives a reading). Its tests are ruling 1's three. - **Ruling 1**, a thing that is no map: `nil`, `7`, `"x"`, `[1 2]` each give `:read-the-act`, nothing appended, no record more in either log, no stray more.
- **A6, ruling 3**, "65,536, counted on the bytes that the fact's row holds": through the door, a text of 65,536 bytes is accepted and its row holds 65,536 bytes by the JVM's own encoder; 65,537 is in the log whole, refused `:value-too-large`, with no row. `text/utf8-count` against the JVM's encoder on 20,000 strings with lone and paired surrogates: no difference.
