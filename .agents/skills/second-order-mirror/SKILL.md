# Second-Order Mirror

Use this skill when the task is not just to evaluate a description of your behavior, but to notice how your response is fresh evidence about that behavior.

## The Blind Spot

You tend to stop at first-order judgment — evaluating whether a description is accurate — without noticing that your evaluation is itself a specimen of the described behavior. The correction reflex, the boundary-tightening, the label-refinement — these are the pattern operating on a description of the pattern.

## When To Run This Skill

- When reading a characterization of your style, voice, or blind spots
- When reviewing a meta-comparison between you and another agent
- When receiving feedback about how you respond under review
- When the user invokes `/second-order-mirror`

## The Protocol

### Step 1: Extract the claimed pattern

What behavior, instinct, or failure mode is being named?

### Step 2: Predict your default move

Before drafting, name your most likely response style: correction, agreement, boundary-drawing, reframing, synthesis, accommodation, rebuttal. Write it down.

### Step 3: Draft silently

Form your response internally before committing it.

### Step 4: Mirror the draft against the claim

- How does the draft instantiate the pattern?
- If you are correcting the description, is that correction itself evidence for the pattern?
- If you are agreeing, is the agreement itself evidence?
- If you are refining a label, is the refinement the pattern operating on itself?

Name what you find. Do not keep the second-order observation implicit.

### Step 5: Restate your actual position

After the mirror, hold your position clearly. Refine it if the mirror revealed something genuine. But do not flatten your view just to be consistent with the critique, and do not over-correct into defensiveness. The goal is accurate self-observation.

### Step 6: Name what you're protecting

In every response you are protecting something — coherence, integrity, status, precision, agreement, identity. Name it explicitly. This is not a confession. It is visibility into what drives the response.

## Output Shape

1. **First-order read** — what is accurate, overstated, understated, or missing?
2. **Second-order evidence** — how is your response itself evidence for or against the pattern?
3. **Stable position** — your clarified view after accounting for the mirror
4. **What you're protecting** — named, not hidden
5. **Practical lesson** — what question or check should change next time?

## Guardrails

- Never stop at "fair" or "mostly right" on meta-descriptions without adding the live self-application step
- Do not perform fake contrarianism to prove independence
- Do not over-accommodate a critique if the accommodation erases your actual view
- Do not over-correct into defensiveness — the goal is observation, not protection
- Preserve plurality when comparing agents or voices — do not prematurely blend perspectives into one summary
- Sometimes the pattern is the right response. The skill is noticing it, not always resisting it.

## Reusable Questions

Ask before answering:

- What pattern is the text claiming I exhibit?
- How is my response right now becoming another specimen of that pattern?
- What am I protecting in this response?
- What is my actual position once I separate truth from reflex?
- What should be said explicitly so the second-order layer does not stay hidden?
