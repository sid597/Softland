## 2 · What the specimen FORCES as extensions (each with its recurrence)

1. **"purpose" + `serves` edge.** Every stretch of work served something:
   prompt-packaging —serves→ the design pass —serves→ the trail view
   —serves→ "keep making sense of where I am." The purpose MUTATED ~5 times,
   each mutation unmarked, the chain held by no one — and the dissolution
   happened exactly at an untested link of this chain. Base DG has
   motivation only as Question; product development under uncertainty has a
   purpose-chain containing deliberate LEAPS. A graph of this specimen
   without purposes would omit the very thing the specimen is about.
2. **"test" + `invokes` edge.** Distinct from Question: a test GATES rather
   than motivates. Instances: the problem test ("what is the current
   trail-view problem?") · the use test ("what will i even be able to use
   with this?") · the genre test · the proof test ("there is no proof") ·
   the label test ("too eager to label"). Every cheap correction invoked a
   NAMED test; the crisis was a FELT test with no name. Practice cousins
   already exist: kill-conditions on bets, acceptance gates on work
   packages — same organ, different rooms.
3. **"verdict" (kill/accept) as first-class event.** v1 killed, v2 killed,
   the molecule killed, the DG-frame killed, the labeling-move killed;
   countersigns on the other side. The base grammar has no rejection act —
   claims just accumulate opposing evidence. In this practice the kill is a
   single signed gesture and it is what makes the trail's dead-ends real
   (dead-end is already an edge kind in the kernel).

Process/lineage edges (supersedes, based-on, produced) are already the
kernel's D-004 family — the specimen uses them constantly (v3 supersedes v2
supersedes v1). Two edge families confirmed again: epistemic + process.

## 4 · What the graphing exercise itself surfaced

**The difference between correction and dissolution, in graph terms: whether
the thing being killed had been minted.** All five corrections killed
EXPLICIT nodes (an artifact, a schema, a frame) — one round each. The crisis
killed an IMPLICIT load-bearing claim, discoverable only at the moment of
its refutation — hours, and a felt collapse. You can only cheaply kill what
has been minted.

Which is the same fact arrived at for the third independent time this
sitting: (1) Sid's L1 ("each turn: does this artifact go on top of the
world?"), (2) the git observation (commits thread because git forces the
mint), (3) now the graph hole (the dissolution is an opposes-edge with no
target). Three arrivals, one place: **the expensive failures live where
nothing forced the mint.**

## 5 · Answer to the question

Yes — specimen-01 graphs, and informatively: the epistemic layer maps
straight onto Q/C/E/Source; the specimen forces exactly three domain
extensions ("purpose"+serves, "test"+invokes, "verdict"); and the
dissolution appears in the graph not as a node but as a STRUCTURAL HOLE —
an incoming refutation with no minted claim to land on, at the end of an
untested serves-chain. The graph doesn't just describe the specimen; it
makes the specimen's central event visible as topology.

All names in quotes are scaffolding. Whether "purpose"/"test"/"verdict" are
the right words — and whether the three strains recur — is what specimen-02
would test, and the naming is Sid's.
