  ★ Insight ─────────────────────────────────────
  This brief is essentially asking: what if code review wasn't reading a 
  document, but navigating a knowledge graph? The key tension is between the
  linearity of reading (how humans consume) and the graph structure of reasoning
   (how decisions actually form). The wildest UI would make the graph feel
  natural to traverse — like walking through a building, not parsing a tree.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Here's my vision — a Reasoning Constellation on an infinite WebGPU canvas with
   semantic zoom. I'll show it at three zoom levels, because progressive
  disclosure IS the UI:

    CMD+K ──────────── Fuzzy search across all claims/evidence/decisions
    ESC ────────────── Zoom out one level (portal → thread → orbit)
    TAB ────────────── Next step in guided traversal

  ★ Insight ─────────────────────────────────────
  The common thread across all 10 visions:

  1. Every design makes GAPS visible — whether it's hollow rock, dark neurons,
  cold cases, dead branches, or NO-GO subsystems. The most powerful feature
  isn't showing what's there — it's making what's MISSING impossible to ignore.
  2. Every design has a "verdict gate" — you can't approve until conditions are
  met. The Crime Board needs all leads resolved. The Metro needs all stations
  clear. Mission Control needs all subsystems GO. This turns review from "read
  and click approve" into "achieve confidence."
  3. Metaphor choice changes behavior — the Newspaper encourages speed-reading.
  The Courtroom encourages adversarial rigor. The Tree encourages nurturing. The
   Metro encourages systematic traversal. The right metaphor for your team IS
  the product decision.
  4. All of these are buildable on your stack — WebGPU gives you the canvas.
  Rama gives you the event log. Missionary gives you the reactivity. The only
  question is: which metaphor best serves the cognitive flow of a developer
  doing review?
