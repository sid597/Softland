## How the terminus pluralizes (session 1, Part II)

> But its closing move — the system's one designated outside "held by the human who signs" — is quietly load-bearing on an assumption that is true today and false at the destination: **n = 1**. Authority terminates at humans, plural. When the second human arrives — and the whole thesis is that many arrive — the terminus is no longer a person but a *body*, and bodies need decision rules, and decision rules are more law, and the regress the clause parked outside the system walks right back in wearing politics. This one, no essay round can close, because it isn't a reasoning gap — it's the oldest open problem our species has, and Softland should refuse to pretend an architecture solves it. The honest constitutional stance: the system achieves full closure over every *machine* judge; among humans it provides the record, the leases, and optionally governance *vocabularies* — quorums, sign-offs, votes as ordinary rows for communities that want them — while remaining agnostic about which politics any commons adopts. The terminus is a seat, and the seat is currently yours; how it pluralizes is a decision that arrives with the second human, and reserving it explicitly is more truthful than the fork's elegant singular.

## The right to unsay (session 1, Part II)

> One more reserved seat, smaller, from stress-testing clause 1 against human dignity: total provenance *forever* eventually meets the thing someone needs not merely superseded but *unsaid* — the personal harm case, not the wrong-claim case. Known machinery exists (redactable payloads under undeniable act-existence — the record keeps that something was said and by whom, while the what becomes provably removable), so this is a ruling to make, not research to do. Named here so the constitution's proudest clause carries its one honest asterisk.
