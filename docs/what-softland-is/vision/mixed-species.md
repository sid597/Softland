## The asymmetric constitution (session 1, Part I, clause 6)

> **6 · A mixed-species constitution.** *(New in its explicitness.)* The two kinds of mind are constitutionally asymmetric, and the law should say so rather than discover it in court. Humans originate ends, bear risk, and are the termini of all authority. Agents supply volume, search, verification, and repair — and their authority is always *leased*: scoped, budgeted, expiring, revocable, on the record. Credit flows in lineages — a mind's credit includes its agents' work. Under this clause, the oversight identity stops being an aspiration and becomes bookkeeping: oversight is what the substrate does when it's just running. Seeing is the substrate's job; *governing* — grant and revocation of the leash — is the one clause that stays irreducibly human. (This clause is North-adjacent; it's my belief offered as input, and that register belongs to you.)

## Not oracles — enzymes (session 1, Part I, from clause 4)

> […] **resident repair crews raise R** — agents that walk your derivations when the ground shifts and mend or flag them, the proofreading enzymes by which biology itself raised its error threshold. The destination is not a maximally frozen base; it's a commons that can afford *living* foundations because repair is a native service. This, more than anything, is what agent minds are *for* in Softland: not oracles — enzymes.

## The human ceiling (session 5, Part I)

> Take your Kardashev paragraph as engineering and it hands you one specific constraint: when agent fanout goes wide, the ceiling of the whole loop is not compute and not storage — it's the rate at which explored territory becomes *humanly legible*. Simon, 1971: a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention. So compression is not a feature of Softland; it is the load-bearing wall of the entire "humans are in the loop of the RSI" claim. And compression only counts if it's checkable — compression is understanding, but only *auditable* compression accumulates; unaccountable summary is precisely the failure mode of the current AI moment. The native that follows: every briefing is itself a claim, with basis edges into what it compressed, challengeable like anything else in the commons. A summary that can be wrong in an addressable way. Views that are citizens of the epistemics they view. The system's real throughput metric falls out of this too: not acts landed per day but something like *basis-freshness of the active minds* — a commons where everyone's basis trails the head by a year has spectacular throughput and is dead.

## Oversight is what the substrate does (session 5, Part I)

> Then the identity I find most consequential in this whole space, said plainly: the natives your hypothesis demands for many *humans* — provenance on every act, visible basis, cheap challenge, authority as data — are exactly the oversight machinery the safety world is trying to invent for many *agents*. Scalable oversight is a concurrency-of-minds problem in which some minds are fast, cheap, and slightly alien. If the concurrency law is right, Softland doesn't need a safety layer bolted on — oversight is what the substrate already does. "Humans can see and have to make sense of all of it" is an alignment strategy stated as a product requirement.

## The designed outside (session 5, Part II)

> And the Gödel caveat from the first essay still binds: no closure completes. Something must stand outside — the stamping kernel, and above it, the constitution's own amendment authority. The fork's clause 6 already placed that outside, I think without noticing it had solved this: **authority terminates at humans.** The tower's unavoidable incompleteness is parked, deliberately, at the species that bears the risk. That is what alignment *is* in this architecture — not a property proven about the system, but a choice about where the system's necessary outside lives. Softland's Gödel sentence is held by you.
