The Simpler Engine

An adversarial architecture review of render-engine-map.md and engine-two-arrows.md, run through three modeled lenses — Jeff Dean (systems & numbers), Dustin Getz (the compiler & the wire), Léo Noel (the flow algebra) — and then pressed on the hardest question: is this the architecture it should be, and can it be simpler for where Softland wants to be?

The verdict in one sentence: the bones are right and earned; the weight sits in the wrong place — the most complex stratum is the least receipt-backed thing in the engine, the real risk lives in a wire that doesn't exist yet, and a simpler engine is available without touching a single settled law except one, named below, which is Sid's call.

Round two (the review run adversarially against itself) survived its own attack with one self-caught omission — the optimistic lane is missing from the fill and from this review's first pass alike (H7) — and two sharpenings: proportionality belongs at the wire, not re-solved in the frame middle (H1); and 60 Hz gestures never touch the batch road at all, so the batch-first bet rides only on act-cadence work (H1).

Round three — the probes ran (this sandbox, Clojure 1.12.4 built from source, the real production fns): P1 killed H1's batch-first proposal by its own pre-registered rule. The batch middle costs 29 ms at 300 entries and 67 ms at 1k on the JVM — 10–40× over the napkin — while the maintained road handles a one-entry change in ~0.6–0.75 ms. The maintained-view stratum now holds the receipt it lacked; the settled law stands unamended. What the probes found instead: three population-bound residues in the live road, priced in H8, and a pick that is receipted fine (P3). Full numbers in §7.

Round four — held against where we want to be (North · the ladder · the spatial model · DIRECTION · the six conflicts, read primary): the measured ceiling and North compose — semantic zoom is the reconciliation, and conflict 5's "millions exist, hundreds resident" now has its numbers. One self-correction: round two's demand-v1 position drifted from the settled road and is restated as a stage with its replacement named. One new connection: H8's residues are pre-priced suspects for the lived "lag grows and grows" friction, feeding road 1a. Full check in §8.

Register. This page holds positions and receipts, not settled ground — contest it. The three lenses are my modeling of these people's published stances, not their actual opinions. Every decision-changing claim carries a file:line anchor; structural claims were spot-checked against source in one bounded wave (working tree, 2026-08-23). What outranks this page: the code, decisions.md, Sid's word.

1 · The verdict in twelve lines

  1. The bones are right and earned. Store as one keyed value · order as row data · tape paint-forward / pick-reverse · CPU pick over the very value the GPU was fed · the shaper as sole text authority · families as data with a pure/GPU split · containers composed in-shader · load-time input fences. Each has a receipt or a scar behind it. None should move.
  2. The weight distribution is wrong. The maintained-view stratum — minted-delta consumers, the semantic reducer, effect view, plan view, oracle twins — is the most law-dense code in the engine and the only major stratum with no measured win attached.
  3. Every measured win on file came from three cheaper disciplines: cadence typing (camera at the sink, blink out of the combine), one memoized text authority (the 28 s frame), and camera-as-one-uniform — the single best decision in the engine.
  4. One doc claim falsified: the oracle twins are flag-gated, not every-frame (renderer.cljs:3554). The double-road cost is carry cost — code weight, goldens, mental load — not frame time. engine-two-arrows §9 and Figure 4 overclaim it; render-engine-map §5.E has it right.
  5. Dean lens: proportionality machinery was built before the population was measured — so round three measured it. The napkin was wrong by 10–40×: the batch middle costs 29 ms at 300 entries JVM, and the maintained stratum earns its keep at 40–73× over batch (P1, ran 2026-08-23). Numbers beat napkins in both directions — that is the lens's own law working.
  6. Getz lens: Figure 4's own caption admits the hand-wired DAG. The keyed wire is the real product. The SSE-first courier position survives — on the condition that demand v1 is explicit membership and the fence is a conformance suite, not a frozen format.
  7. Noel lens: the scars were continuous/discrete typing errors, not reactivity errors. The crank is ten lines of Missionary; the scheduler should fold into it as the pure decide, never stand beside it as a second event system.
  8. Eight hard questions (§4): the unreceipted stratum (H1 — since resolved against this review by its own probe) · fork debt · the dead frame function · the custody contradiction between the two docs · Region3D pre-product · the wire with zero receipts · the optimistic lane the first pass missed (H7) · and what the probes found instead: three priced population-bound residues in the live road (H8).
  9. The simpler engine (§5) survives round three at half strength: forks close, the dead surface goes, Region3D freezes, the wire comes first — but the maintained middle stays: P1 killed the batch-first half, and the deletion list shrinks accordingly.
  10. The deciding instance is not in the engine. The Block slice is the courier probe, the engine's re-founding consumer, and draw-frame!'s first runtime caller. Build it before touching the engine further.
  11. The destination check (round four): the measured ~1–2k-entry ceiling and North compose — semantic zoom band-swaps representations, so "millions exist, hundreds are resident" now carries receipts, and the residency law gets its first number. One drift self-corrected: demand v1 restated as a stage whose named replacement is the settled road's own "cost scales with what you're looking at."
  12. The measurements reached back into a lived friction: Sid's "lag grows and grows as I write more" has the exact signature of the two per-swap O(N) mechanisms round three priced (hop 5 · effective) — pre-registered suspects handed to road 1a, behind its banked-receipt fence.

2 · One falsification finding first verified against source

engine-two-arrows overclaims the oracle cost. §9 says the batch tape compiler runs beside the maintained arrangement "every frame", and Figure 4 draws it that way. The code disagrees: compile-frame-tape's only call site is inside frame-tape-twin-check! (renderer.cljs:3557), and that body — tape twin, effect-view oracle, plan-view oracle, all of it — sits behind a single flag, __softland_frame_tape_twin_check (renderer.cljs:3554), the flag's only occurrence in src and test.

Consequence: the double-road cost is carry cost — code weight, goldens, mental load — not frame-time cost. That recalibrates §9's "wasteful" ledger, and it matters for what "simpler" should mean: the case against the maintained stratum is complexity and receipt-absence, never runtime waste. render-engine-map §5.E ("behind a flag, the twin check…") is correct; the two-arrows §9 bullet and Figure 4 label should repair in place at the next fresh-eyes round.

Also verified in the same wave: draw-frame! has zero real callers and the verifier never even names it (only the .mjs fence greps its source text) · derive-store-frame walks the full slot vector ~15 times per call, one pass per output lane, no memo (scene_store.cljc:321-376) · requestAnimationFrame has zero hits in kept src · apply-assembly's sole caller is scene_store/build-face-tree (scene_store.cljc:513), called twice from scene_runtime.

3 · The three lenses

Modeled stances — what each discipline's founder would plausibly say, from their published work; not their words.

Jeff Dean — numbers before machinery

systems · latency · napkin math

What this lens sees immediately: the two hottest cost claims in the architecture have no measured magnitude, and the docs admit it (§11 "not settled": derive-store-frame O(N) cost unknown; pick cost is one swiftshader quote). Yet the most complex stratum exists precisely to make recompute proportional to change. That is optimization ahead of the profile.

Read the scars table with a cost-accountant's eye — every measured win came from three cheap disciplines, and not one is attributable to the maintained-view stratum:

ScarMeasuredWhat actually fixed it
the camera watch8–26 fps → 45–60 fpscadence: camera deref'd at the rAF sink only
caret-blink reshape1440 ms long-task / 530 mscadence: stable → overlay chains, one m/signal

Region3D shape-mint (open)1974 comparator calls / 1 entryunfixed — and it lives inside the incremental machinery

The napkin: a product canvas is maybe 10²–10³ slots, ops already flattened per slot. A batch re-derive of tape + spans + plan at that population, gated so it only runs when something changed, is plausibly single-digit milliseconds — hypothesis, not verdict; kill-probe P1 below. If it holds, the maintained stratum is a 100× design carried before the 1× product exists.

What this lens praises without reservation: the ledger/receipts/replay culture, load-time fences (failing at load for a forbidden input beats any review note), goldens-as-hashes, and camera-as-one-uniform — the common case costs nothing.

Dustin Getz — you rebuilt the DAG by hand, and the wire is the real product

compiler-derived reactivity · network transparency

Figure 4's own caption is the thesis: "that is the shape Electric builds for you from code. Here every node is hand-wired." Seven touch-sites per new view is the assembly-language tax of a reactive graph without a compiler. Two exits exist — adopt a compiler, or make view registration fully data (families already are {:contract :inputs :produce :execute!} rows; the semantic views aren't). There is a third exit the doc doesn't name: stop maintaining, and there is nothing to wire — see H1.

On transport this lens co-signs §7a completely: Electric-as-RPC was the anti-pattern; the keyed wire is the point. Where it contests the doc is the courier POSITION (SSE-first): demand propagation is exactly what the compiler gives you free — hand-rolling feed + broker + watermark + reconnect + backpressure is rebuilding Electric's core competence as an owned lane, and "the courier is a one-file swap" is usually a fiction, because couriers grow semantics (ordering, catch-up, reconnect), and that is where the bugs live.

Adjudication: the SSE-first position survives, on one condition — demand v1 is explicit membership (the containers a lens has open), not renderer-published visibility facts. That collapses the broker to a subscription parameter, defers the thrash-prone part (visibility-driven residency) until a memory or bandwidth receipt demands it, and shrinks the courier surface to something a conformance suite can actually pin. The fence must be that suite — ordering · watermark resume · two-edits-one-tick · tombstone linger, run against any courier — not "the format is frozen". A frozen format doesn't freeze semantics. (Round four correction: the deferral half of this drifted from the settled road's "cost scales with what you're looking at" — restated in §8 as stage 1 with visibility-driven residency as the named replacement; and the suite turns out to re-derive DIRECTION 1c's own stability tests.)

Léo Noel — the scars are typing errors, not reactivity errors

missionary · continuous vs discrete · supervision

The scars table reads as "ambient reactivity is dangerous". The algebra reads it differently: blink, camera, and diamonds were continuous/discrete typing errors — a 60 Hz or 530 ms discrete signal placed as an input to a wide continuous combine. Missionary's whole design is that cadence is typed: continuous (m/latest/m/signal, sampled) versus discrete (m/ap/m/reduce, backpressured). The architecture discovered "camera at the sink, sample once per frame" empirically; the algebra states it a priori. So this lens blesses the current shape — pure core, atoms at edges, three named m/latest, sample at rAF is idiomatic — but corrects the moral: the danger was never "everything reactive", it was "cadence untyped".

Two sharper pokes. The engine-with-no-crank is not a gap: the crank is ~10 lines (m/reduce over m/sample of the world at >raf), which the deleted host already proved. Don't let the scheduler grow to compensate — its seven causes + deadline registry + replay is a second event system standing beside the flow algebra; small (7.9K) and tested, so keep it, but as the pure decide inside the host's fold, never a parallel machine. And the delta journal + ack watermark is a hand-built discrete flow with backpressure; when the host returns, delivery is m/observe → m/reduce — keep the minting at owners (undo and the wire need those values), let the algebra do delivery.

4 · The adversarial pass — eight hard questions

H1–H6 are round one, against the docs. H7 is round two — the review run against itself. H8 is round three — what the probes found when they ran; H1's resolution block records the reversal.

H1 · The maintained stratum has no receipt — and it's the complexity center refuted by P1 · round three

Resolution first, argument after. P1 ran (round three, §7). The batch middle measured 29 ms at 300 entries and 67 ms at 1k on the JVM — far past this claim's own pre-registered bar (≤4 ms at 2k) — while the maintained road handles a one-entry change in ~0.6–0.75 ms with oracle-equality confirmed on every run. H1-strong is withdrawn; the maintained-view stratum now holds the receipt it lacked; no amendment to the seam law is proposed. The argument below is kept as the record of what was claimed and why; what survives of it is the wire-population synthesis (round two, ①) — now quantified: with these constants a lens must hold roughly ≤1–2k entries for the engine to breathe, so demand-scoped delivery is load-bearing, not optional. The residues the probe found instead are in H8.

The biggest structural claim of this review: frame_delta consumers + frame_semantic_state + frame_effect_view + frame_plan_view (≈38K of the most law-dense code) plus the renderer's maintained arrangement exist for a proportionality no measurement has yet shown the product needs. The batch siblings — compile-frame-tape, derive-effect-spans, compile-frame-plan — are already written, already trusted (they're the oracles), and already sit behind changed-families (which stays: cheap and load-fenced).

The inversion available: promote the oracle to the only road. Batch-recompute the frame's small structures whenever the gate says something changed; keep keyed uploads; keep delta minting at owners. Your own law contains this move's mirror — "a batch stage is never deleted when its incremental sibling arrives; it is demoted to that sibling's oracle." The adversarial completion: an incremental sibling is never kept without a receipt that batch fails. Same principle, both directions: batch is truth; incremental pays rent.

One honest cost: this relaxes the letter of "recompute proportional to change, never to the population" (the batch walks the frame's population below the gate) while keeping its spirit — nothing runs when nothing changed; no read-side rediscovery of write-known facts; deltas still name what changed. That is an amendment to settled ground.

Round two — this claim attacked, three results. ① The population objection dissolves at the wire. The strongest case for the maintained stratum is "where we want to be is unbounded — whole lands, spaces in spaces, not 10³ slots." But the fill already contains the answer: demand-scoped delivery (membership) bounds what enters the client store before the frame ever sees it. The maintained stratum and the demand-scoped feed are the same proportionality promise made at two different layers — duplicate machinery. Solve proportionality once, at the wire, where residency law lives anyway; then batch-first below it is safe by construction, not just by bench. ② Gestures never touch the batch road. A pan is one camera uniform; a container drag is one 32-byte row rewrite, a :parameter delta by design ("a slider drag never re-plans passes"), and "camera and container motion stay shader values" is the families' own upload law — so the 60 Hz path bypasses tape, spans, and plan entirely. The batch road runs at act cadence (a keystroke, an insert), not frame cadence. Structure-from-docs, not measured. ③ The one scar that looks like a receipt for maintained order isn't. The per-frame tape-recompile scar's disease was the tape keyed on [:frame idx] — the clock as ancestor, recompiling unconditionally every rAF. Batch-first behind the gate never runs on an unchanged frame; and the store-level :ordered map that fix produced is kept here regardless (pick reads it). The scar indicts clock-keying, not batch-per-change.

Kill-probe P1 — RAN, round three: the pre-registered rule was "under ~2 ms at product scale → the stratum moves to LATER; over → the stratum finally has its receipt, and this claim is withdrawn." It measured 29 ms at 300 entries. Withdrawn. The Figma corroboration line above is retracted with it — dirty-flags-plus-re-render works there on C++ arrays; Softland's persistent-structure constants (~30 μs/entry for spans alone) are two orders heavier, and that difference, not the algorithm, decides the road. Full numbers §7.

H2 · Fork debt is the second complexity center sid call

Two text roads (MSDF + Slug), two render roads (legacy + linear compositor), a legacy monospace layout road, a color seam default-off. Each fork was lawful at birth; jointly they are most of why the engine "reads as tons of code" (§1.14's own words). Several are "off until an activation receipt" — a receipt no product exists to produce, so they are unpriced options with no expiry.

Close them by naming the trigger now: one text road live, the other in git; legacy render road as v1, compositor activates with the first product effect. Goldens thin accordingly.

The text-road decision rule, sharpened (round two): the choice is the spatial model's, not typography's. If text must stay crisp across the ratified zoom envelope (z0.01–z1000) inside nested spaces, Slug is the survivor — exact per-pixel coverage from outlines is the only road that doesn't degrade — and MSDF goes to git. If text at extreme zoom may honestly degrade or fade under residency law like any other material, MSDF is the survivor and Slug's 100-byte glyphs go. One line from Sid decides which world Softland is.

H3 · The most integrative function in the engine is dead code guarded by a grep verified

draw-frame! (~330 lines — the one place inputs → gate → produce → arrange → reduce → plan → encode composes) has zero callers, and the verifier never executes it — it composes its own frame path, with a static .mjs fence asserting the two roads stay twins textually (verify_scene_tape_fence.mjs:50 extracts the source as a string; verifier.cljs has zero hits for it). Every claim about "the frame" is currently structural.

Either the verifier drives draw-frame! directly or the Block host does, soon. Corollary worth saying plainly: the verifier (304K, bigger than the renderer at 203K) is the area's only consumer, so engine shape drifts toward verifiability-at-rest. The counterweight isn't less verifier — it's a product host.

H4 · The custody contradiction between the two docs contradictionsid call

render-engine-map marks the presentation grammar (face_assembly + face_primitives, 93K) as one-cut deletion debt. engine-two-arrows' fill (§8, hop 3) builds the face tree per unit "from the served assembly (16 builders now)" — and the spot-check confirms apply-assembly → build-face-tree → scene_runtime is the only face→tree path in the tree (scene_store.cljc:513 · scene_runtime.cljs:285,514). If custody executes before the Block slice, the slice rebuilds the thing that was just deleted.

Likely resolution: the grammar is product — it is the agent-authoring surface and the only path from served data to boxes; the unused primitives are the debt. Either way the two docs currently disagree, and that needs one line from Sid before any cut.

H5 · Region3D is engineering carried at interest, pre-product sid call

≈119K of material + ≈107K of GPU for a family with goldens and no host. Sid's own words on the dossier: "that is not product that is eng." The boundary — a whole 3D scene as one tape row — is genuinely good and costs nothing to keep frozen. Freeze at goldens; no further investment until a product face needs a region. The one open perf item (shape-mint, 1974 comparator calls per entry) waits with it — and note it lives inside the incremental machinery H1 questions.

H6 · The architecture question can't be answered from the engine side at all direction

Both docs converge on this and the adversarial pass confirms it: every engine piece has goldens; the wire — demand up, keyed delivery down, act-correlated apply — has zero receipts in either direction, and it is the layer where the two settled instincts (keyed all the way; one reactive system) actually live or die. Polishing the engine further is attractor-following.

The Block slice (§8's "smallest slice", with its three tripwires) is not just the courier probe — it is the engine's re-founding consumer, draw-frame!'s first runtime caller, and the only instance that can say whether the whole shape is right.

H7 · The optimistic lane is missing — from the fill, and from this review's first pass self-caught · round two

The loop as drawn — in two-arrows Figure 6, in the after-table's hop rows, and in this page's own Figure 2, round one — routes a durable act as: edit → write route → Rama → accepted → the feed → the store → pixels. Read that literally and every keystroke's pixel waits on a server round-trip. Road 1a's whole promise was "typing stops lagging"; the fill as written re-creates the lag by construction and none of its three tripwires would catch it — "one edit moves exactly one slot" passes whether the slot moves in 8 ms or 300.

The repair is a named lane, and the project's own golden rule already mandates its shape: never approve an optimistic-state change without tracing the full write → render → truth-reconciliation → clear lifecycle. Concretely: the client host applies the act locally as a pending overlay keyed by act-id — rendered same-frame, carried beside (never inside) the served truth — and the feed's echo, correlated by the same act-id the fill already carries ("correlation = act-id; caps dissolve"), reconciles and clears it. Session-state machinery for gestures already exists; acts have no such lane anywhere in the fill.

Consequence for the contract: the Block-slice contract must decide the optimistic lane explicitly (pending overlay vs server-echo-only typing — the second is a product decision to accept RTT in the keystroke path, which nobody has made), and the slice gains a fourth tripwire: keystroke-to-pixel is local-frame-fast, and the pending mark clears on the act's echo — never before, never twice.

H8 · What the probes found instead — three priced residues and one heavy truth measured · round three

The heavy truth first: the middle's constants are enormous, everywhere. ~30 μs per entry for batch effect spans; ~15 μs per entry for tape validate+sort; ~12 μs per slot for the store-frame projection; ~28 μs per container for effective; 70–340 μs per slot upsert on the write side. This is the price of persistent-structure allocation, and it — not any algorithm — is why batch loses and why every road, maintained included, has a population ceiling. The fused single-walk derive-store-frame experiment proved it: byte-equal output, not faster (12.4 vs 12.0 ms at 1k) — the cost is allocation, not the ~15 walks, so round one's "fused to one, cheap regardless" is retracted; the real fix for hop 5 is the seam ruling's own per-key views.

The three population-bound residues in the live, receipted road:

  • project-spans is O(N) per dirty frame inside the maintained road — it rebuilds a full index over the arrangement on every call (frame_effect_view.cljc:249-252): measured 274 μs at 300 entries, 485 μs at 1k, 2.33 ms at 5k. The docs state the structure ("walked once"); nobody had priced it. At 5k entries the "incremental" road pays real frame budget here — the dominant maintained-road line.
  • containers/effective is a from-scratch batch recompute in the live path — 28 ms at 1k containers, 170 ms at 5k, per registry change (the <effective view is one m/latest over the registry watch). Since a drag mutates the registry, this is a scaling cliff no doc names: fine at ~100 containers (~3 ms), jank at 1k. [Magnitude measured; the drag-cadence link is structure-from-docs, not traced live.]
  • derive-store-frame (hop 5) at 12 μs/slot per store swap — 13.8 ms at 1k slots per edit echo, at act cadence. Already named by the seam ruling as the whole-population step; now it has a number, and fusion is proven not to be the fix.

What this adds up to: the engine's proportionality law is honored by the arrangement and the views (receipted, H1) but leaks at these three points — and the wire-population bound from round two graduates from synthesis to requirement: with these constants, demand-scoped delivery keeping a lens at ≤1–2k entries is what makes the whole engine viable. Board debt, not Sid calls: per-key store views (hop 5), an incremental or memoized effective, and a maintained span index that doesn't rebuild per frame.

5 · Is this the architecture it should be? Can it be simpler?

The bones — right, earned, untouched

  • the store as one keyed value; one swap = one generation
  • order as row data; tape paint-forward / pick-reverse
  • CPU pick over the very value the GPU was fed — agents and the mouse share one finger
  • the shaper as the sole text authority (the floor; best-earned piece in the engine)
  • families as data, material/GPU split, no branch in the core walkers
  • containers as one storage table composed in-shader; camera as one uniform
  • load-time input fences — clock and raw camera refused at load
  • delta minting at owners (serves undo and the wire regardless of H1)
  • the goldens + receipts + replay culture (thinned to live roads)

Every one of these has a receipt or a scar behind it. The architecture is not wrong — its weight distribution is: complexity sits in an unmeasured incremental stratum, open forks kept warm, a dead host surface, and one pre-product family, while the actual risk sits in a wire that doesn't exist.

The bet underneath, examined and held

Round two also pressed the question every other question stands on, the one round one took as given: should this be a WebGPU-native substrate at all, versus DOM + Electric for the 95% (blocks, documents, text) with GPU islands for the rest? The §2 organs table is honest that Softland carries a browser-engine slice of its own — that is the whole cost side. Examined, the bet holds, for a product reason and an economic one: the product is the spatial substrate — one camera over everything, spaces inside spaces as first-class faces, agents picking through the same finger as the mouse, faces as data with no DOM to leak through — and a DOM Softland is a different product, not a cheaper build of this one. Economically, the expensive organ that makes GPU text viable — the shaper, the floor, the best-earned piece in the tree — is already paid for and fenced. Re-litigating the substrate now would discard the one sunk cost that was worth sinking. Held: not assumed, examined.

The simpler engine, end to end

Deleted or frozen vs today (revised by round three): one text road · one render road · scheduler folded into the crank as decide · Region3D frozen at goldens · the dead host surface's uncalled fns. The maintained arrangement / effect-view / plan-view — struck from the list: P1 receipted them. The fused-hop-5 line is struck too (equal, not faster); hop 5's fix is per-key views (H8).

The one law it would have touched: none, any more. Round one proposed relaxing "recompute proportional to change, never to the population" below the gate; P1 measured why the law is right at product populations on this substrate. The law stands unamended, now with a receipt where it previously had only scars.
