What the code does today, from its own docs: a region is a whole 3D scene as closed declared data, objects with transforms, meshes, shading and lights, a camera, a background, a 2D extent. It renders offscreen into a leased texture and is composited into the 2D frame as one ordered entry, and it answers picks on the CPU from its own spatial index. One file, on_plane, puts text and ink onto object-local planes inside it, and it is the only place two kinds meet. That is the state, not the answer.

Working basis I have adopted and want attacked rather than assumed. From the path round: define what the thing must mean before deciding how it is drawn; every session that led with rendering made a definitional error, and every session that led with meaning made none. From the field: a 3D scene is objects with transforms, geometry, materials, lights and a camera, and rendering is projection, visibility and shading; the interchange forms are settled and the execution is contested, the same split the 2D imaging model has. And one composition primitive: render a scene of either model into a surface, and a surface is paint in the other model, in both directions and recursively. The 2D picture's contract ends at a region drawn with per-pixel coverage; whether that is also where 3D begins is open.

Questions I am carrying:
- What is the general 3D object, and what are its sources? A modelled mesh, a formula solid, a scan or a splat cloud, a 2D shape given depth, a whole scene. Is the region the thing, or one container of things?
- Containment: 3D inside 2D as today, 2D inside 3D, or nested spaces of both kinds. What must a 3D thing continue to mean after I orbit it, move an object, place ink on a face, zoom the page it sits in, or share it?
- Two cameras: the page's zoom and the region's camera. What composes them, and what does "zoom level will make something happen" mean when the zoom crosses into a region?
- Picking and hover through the seam: click in, "you hit this" out, when the thing hit is ink on a face of an object inside a region on a page.
- Four things 2D never had: a mark's pixels depend on other marks (shadows, occlusion, transparency order); many shape representations at once, and converting between them is the work; 2D and 3D nest both ways, including 2D derived from 3D (sections, silhouettes); ranges and budgets past float32 and per-frame time. Which of these force code below the waist, and which are data above it?
- What must exist as code below the waist for 3D, and what is its input and output, such that the ceilings can be built above it as data? One waist for 2D and 3D, or a reason there are two.
- Am I reasoning from what region3d does today or from how it should be? If the best team in the field were handed this, how would they define the problem, and what trade-offs would they name that I do not know to ask about?
