## What it is

The page (a planar space, px) holds a portal R onto a spatial space S (metres, Y up) through view V. In S: a solid B whose shape is what a saved construction CB evaluates to (a rectangle R0 on the ground, extruded to E, optionally cut by a slab C across the top from back to front); a sphere Q that can pass in front; a directional light; a planar surface Π attached to B's face `E.top` (millimetres, 1000 per metre) carrying a pen stroke k (samples with a width each, the swept round nib), by a saved **attachment rule** that says what the ink preserves when the face changes; and a portal T attached to B's face `E.front` showing a planar land L (a circle, a rect, a label) through view VL. ## The seam walker (what the address means)

`walk(pagePoint)` in the script. Page pixel → page world (the page's pan and zoom are the page's view) → is it inside I? then the answer is a pixel of I and nothing else, no seam. Inside R? → portal-local → the used viewport's pixel → `rayFromRegionPoint` with the same matrices the renderer uses (`cameraOf` serves both) → the nearest of `hitBlocks` (a slab test per evaluated block, each face tagged with its construction face and part) and `hitSphere` → on `E.top`: the attachment resolves (`resolveAttachment`: rule + the face's frame from the evaluation → origin, scale, offset) → `attachUV` → `classifyStroke` (the exact swept nib: the uneven-capsule signed distance per segment, min over segments) → the address ends at k with segment, t, arc length, width, and a lower bound on the distance to the edge; on `E.front`: T's normalized attachment → `hitLand` in L → the address ends at c, r or L's background; on `C.floor` (a face the cut generated): no attachment resolves, the address says so. Every level carries its coordinates. The footprint line on Π projects one Π millimetre and measures it in page pixels: the filler's tolerance on the face, per view, under the attachment rule.

Hover is `hover(reason)` = the walk over `(state.pointer, data, views)`, re-run on pointer move and whenever data or a view changes with the pointer held (`state.pendingHover = 'data' | 'view'`). An edit of the construction or of the attachment rule is data: it re-walks the hover under a still pointer. The diff line says `entered`, `left → entered`, or `still`, and names whether the pointer, the data or the view moved.

**What "agree by construction" covers, and what it does not.** `cameraOf` serving both the renderer and the walker guarantees the coordinate relation between the picture and the hit: a world point lands on the same portal pixel in both. It does not make the two agree about *meaning*: the fold found the renderer and the picker agreeing with each other through a wrong aspect (both read it from the lease) and through a wrong nib (both projected onto the centreline). Meaning is defined by the formulas the walker and the shader each implement and is checked apart: the classify is now the closed-form swept nib on both sides, and the framing is the portal's on both sides.

## The evaluator and the attachment (the fold's addition)

`evaluate(construction, previous)` runs per edit of CB and returns convex blocks whose faces name the construction face they belong to (`E.top`, `E.front`, `E.left`…, `C.floor`, `C.wall-`, `C.wall+`) and which part of it, plus a **lineage** per face against the previous evaluation: `unchanged`, `modified (1.00 → 1.40 m wide)`, `split into 2 by C`, `merged from 2 (the cut was undone)`, `generated by C`, `deleted`. The report goes to the per-edit rate line and the address.

`resolveAttachment(rule, face)` turns the rule and the evaluated face's frame into where Π sits: Π mm = ((x, z) − origin) · scale + offset, × 1000.

| rule | what the ink preserves | scale, offset |
|---|---|---|
| `origin` | its distances from the sketch's origin corner | 1, 0 |
| `normalized` | its proportions of the face | authored extents / current extents, 0 |
| `edge` | its distance from the +x edge | 1, (authored width − current width, 0) |

`onSplit: clip` keeps the authored stroke whole in Π and paints it only where a surviving part of `E.top` is under it (the shader runs the Π material on `E.top` parts only; a generated face gets no ink); `onSplit: detach` leaves k in the record on no face and the address says so. The same resolution feeds the shader's uniforms and the walker: one definition of where Π is. The portal T is an attachment too (`normalized`): widen the rectangle and the land stretches with the face; cut, and the notch cuts the picture.

## The post, the field, the second record and the drawing (the fold of attack 1)

**P's wall and its chart.** `postOf(CP)` gives the wall's authored chart S0: u = arc length in mm from the +x generator, counter-clockwise seen from +Y, v = height in mm; `S0(u, v) = (cx + r cos(u/R), v/1000, cz + r sin(u/R))`, C = 2πR. A saved chart parameter {offset o, scale s} describes the same wall by `S1(a, b) = S0(s·a + o, b)`; the transition φ(u, v) = ((u − o)/s, v), the period in a is C/s, the metric is diag(s², 1). `evaluateP(CP, previous)` returns the support and a **correspondence piece** against the previous evaluation: from (revision, wall, all) to (revision, wall, all), the relation (identity | reparameterization with φ, its metric and period | deformation with the generator and the height kept), forward/backward unique, the evidence (same points | declared deformation), error 0. `resolveWall(rule, evaluation)` turns the attachment's rule into the binding: under a chart change the authored-domain map composes with φ and nothing moves; under a radius change `origin` keeps millimetres from the generator (u kept) and `normalized` keeps proportions of the circumference (u scaled by C′/C). `postChart` maps a world point to the chart's (a, b); `postPreimages` lists the chart point's preimages u = s·a + o + k·C inside the authored domain (at most one for a domain narrower than C; several for content that wraps); `classifyMark` classifies an authored point against the mark with the same `capsuleSD` as k's and returns every segment inside as a contributor, so the self-crossing returns one mark with two source locations. The wall's material (class 4 in the main program) does the same per fragment: θ from atan2, the chart's a, the preimage loop over k ∈ {−1, 0, 1}, the capsule in millimetres on the surface; it also draws the chart's 100-unit grid and its cut (a = 0), so a reparameterization is visible as the grid and the cut moving under a mark that does not. The two wrong readings are the same code with different inputs: `seam=modulo` hands the shader and the walker knots with u reduced modulo C (the first segment's +160 mm becomes −1096.6 mm and the mark runs the other way round the post); `nib=naive` hands them φ-mapped knots and classifies in chart units (the mark half as wide along the wall; a point 25 mm along the circumference reads 12.5 and the naive nib puts it inside where the surface nib puts it 5.37 mm outside).

**F, the field.** `S.things.F` is a formula with parameters and what it supplies; no face. `hitField(ray, F, workLimit)` is the CPU reference: the quartic along the ray (`fieldPoly`), its real roots on the bounds interval by the derivative chain (`isolateRealRoots`: f‴ → f″ → f′ → f, every monotone piece bisected; a critical point where f = 0 is a contact, which a sign-change loop misses; a finite work limit returns the unfinished pieces as unresolved, never as empty space), the events of every leaf merged, the Boolean max(f_outer, −f_inner) ≤ 0 decided between consecutive events, the first material interval's start as the hit with its gradient normal and active leaf. The walker keeps an unresolved result in hand: if its unresolved interval starts before the winner's t, the address says the winner behind it is not definite and the leaf is `F:unresolved`. The GPU draws F's bounds box through a second program (`progF`) whose fragment shader runs the same isolation (`quarticRoots`, unrolled) from the eye through the fragment's world point and writes `gl_FragDepth` from the hit, so F occludes and is occluded like the blocks under the one depth test. No parity read exists yet between the two (fix list).

**k2 and the merge.** `records=2` cuts the top and authors k2 (a preset, 61 samples) in its own planar space Π2 whose origin is the right part's corner (world x 0.30, Π 800 mm), with its own rule. `merge=1` undoes the cut against that evaluation, so `evaluate` reports `merged from 2` and `mergeCorrespondence(previous, evaluated)` returns two restrictions (part 1 → x ∈ [0, 620] mm, part 2 → x ∈ [800, 1000] mm, evidence restricted old support) and the strip with no predecessor (620, 800). Both records bind to the one face: Ftop as before, Fright restricted to x ≥ 800. The shader evaluates both strokes on class-3 fragments (`uStroke2` on its own unit) and composes the overlap by `composition.order` (premultiplied source-over) or hatches it when the order is null; the walker classifies both (`classifyStroke2`) and prints the contributors and `composeAt`'s result, or needs-policy. `left=trimmed` clips k to the old parts' ranges (a destructively trimmed copy), so the strip stays blank after the merge; the default keeps k whole under clip, so it reappears there.

**D, the drawing.** `section=<h>` puts D below the portal, in page world: `sectionOf(h)` returns a rectangle per block the plane crosses (source: the block and its faces), the post's analytic circle (source: P wall ← CP.r), and the field's level curve lowered by 96 samples (marked not analytic). `hitD` returns the nearest curve with its parameter, its distance off and its source relation; for the circle the address adds the dimension's saved reaction (sets CP.r) and says that dragging the loop itself has no unique inverse.
