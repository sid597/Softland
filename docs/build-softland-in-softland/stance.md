## The simple story

1. Building Softland in Softland is not making a copy; it is absorption — moving workshop steps from outside (files, git, IDE, agents-as-file-editors) to inside, one at a time. `[ratified]`
2. A tool never builds itself alone; it builds through a chain of forms, and the tool owns only the last step. The real question is always: what form can Softland grip, and how does its own body get into that form? (Sid's hammer.) `[Sid→ratified]`
3. Softland has one material: facts — held by Rama, moved by Electric, shown by the renderer. The engines are carriage and the loop closes through material only. `[ratified]`
4. Everything sorts into two strata: code, which grows the language, and material, which grows the world. `[ratified]`
5. Four forces pin a thing into code: enactment, floor/repair, heat, trust. Everything else belongs above, as material. `[ratified]`
6. The waist is not one layer: it is a low floor, a composition law, and an open seam. Components live above it as facts, so the component set is never "set." `[ratified]`
7. Things built before the material existed (the block editor, one-atom Electric) are pre-material monoliths: they get melted along the stratum line, not ported or maintained. `[Sid→ratified]`
8. The waist is seeded, then measured: every forced drop below it is a fence-trip naming a missing native; recurrence of the same reach across strata confirms one. Fences are instruments first, walls second. `[ratified]`
9. Views are standing queries over facts — one idea at three clocks (record/Rama, presence/Electric, frame/renderer) — and an agent's context is a fourth face of the same machinery. `[Sid→ratified]`
10. Zoom is semantic: altitude shows named boundaries, and each space's ladder of meanings is material, not geometry. `[Sid→ratified]`
11. Boundaries let law flow down and meaning flow up; containment is the one kernel tree; every other relation is overlay, interpreted by queries. `[ratified]`
12. The stratum treaty recurses up userland: stewards hold grammars others live inside; whole softwares become imports of material — zero lines below the waist. `[ratified]`
13. The substrate can hold anyone's law but cannot say whose law holds. Adjudication between perspectives is the inhabitants' territory — the economy door, not yet opened. `[ratified]`

## Laws and patterns

### L1 — The chain of forms `[Sid→ratified]`
"X builds X" is a fact about a chain, not about X: raw form → transformations → the one grippable form → the tool's final operation. Sid's anchors: *hammer, wood and iron, "malleable at the point of my hammer," the workshop.* The analogy's admitted break is the hope: software's workshop can be absorbed into the thing; a hammer's never can.

### L2 — The material law `[ratified]`
One material: facts (Rama-held, Electric-moved, renderer-shown). The self-building loop runs through material only — engines touch nothing else. So the body must become material AND the body-as-material must get a face. The anti-"slap on some UI" test: **the thing that shows the code must be the kind of thing it shows**, so the next look can land on the viewer itself. Decisions live as material too — hard-code 0,0 as the *value* of a position fact, never a constant in render code. First act of self-building: a fact-edit (move/re-wrap the code-face from inside), not a program-edit.

### L3 — The stratum treaty `[ratified]`
A thing can live as data exactly when its meaning is already enactable by existing code. Code grows the language; data grows the world. The kernel holds interpreters, not features. Four pinning forces: **enactment** (facts go dark without it), **floor** (must survive arbitrary data breakage — the tools that repair X cannot live inside X), **heat** (runs-per-second decides whether the interpretation tax is payable; thickness is the human axis, heat the machine axis — Sid's compile-native point, refined `[Sid→ratified]`), **trust** (invariants the inside must not edit away). Existing proof-in-miniature: the facet-master pattern — code floor + grammar + validators below, revisions + wear above. `[checked]`

### L4 — Monolith diagnosis and the melt `[Sid→ratified]`
Pre-material artifacts couldn't be made of the material because it didn't exist yet. Named members of the melt queue, by Sid: the block editor ("should it exist in this form?" — no as-is; it is not the carrier of text) and one-atom Electric ("one atom that takes everything, that is so wrong"). The melt direction is always the same: verbs and physics sink into the kernel; view, state, policy, bindings rise into facts. The editor dissolves entirely: cursor/selection = entities, keymap = facts, behaviors = facets, view = a face, remainder = a dozen primitive edit verbs + the commit artery. The rethink of Electric: transport in the material's grain — fact-level changes, per-thing subscriptions, never replace-everything. (Derived blind; diff against docs/electric-native/ — convergence is evidence, divergence is a finding. `[stance]`)

### L5 — The waist `[ratified]`
Floor + composition law + open seam. Component libraries have the right shape and the wrong stratum: their "set"-ness (Sid's word) comes from components-as-code; components-as-facts grow by inhabitation. Derived from what Softland is for — two kinds of minds building by arranging material while living inside it — forcing: low floor (anything buildable), sayable daily units (assemblies: "move that card"), and a data component-layer (vocabulary grows without Sid's maintenance). Seed guess at the floor `[stance]`: fact-kinds text-run · stroke/shape · image · mesh · connector; verbs insert · delete · move · connect · group. The seed is expected to be wrong in places; the fence corrects it (L6).

### L6 — The fence instrument `[Sid→ratified]`
The signal was never "opened the IDE" — that was its human costume (Sid hasn't opened his IDE in months; agents do the churn). The signal is **forced below the waist**: an intent expressible as arrangement that had to become code. Instrument first, wall second: each crossing records the *reach* (what was reached for, in whose situation); **recurrence of the same reach across strata names a native** (ratified live in-session when "query" arrived independently from Rama, Electric, the UI, and agent-context — Sid: "we are hitting a fence"). The metric: fraction of intents that had to become code, falling over time. Fences themselves are material, not prose — the facet treaty applied to process: boundary as facts, crossings as wear. Pre-Softland forms (skills, CLAUDE.md) are pre-material fences: read by minds, enforced by nothing, invisible, unmetered.

### L7 — Projection: standing queries at every clock `[Sid→ratified]`
Between facts and enactors sits projection: a standing query parameterized by camera, focus, and perspective, deciding which facts, at what grain, in which representation, become this view's material. "It's like a query being run on the visual form" (Sid) is not an analogy — Rama IS standing queries at the record clock, Electric at the presence clock, the ZUI renderer becomes the third at the frame clock, and **an agent's context is a face**: same machinery, different enactor (tokens instead of glyphs; provenance renders as citations; fences render as visible walls). Pointing is the inverse query (pixel → provenance → fact); bidirectionality is writes-to-facts fanning through subscriptions. Kernel: query engine, provenance threading, perspective parameter. Material: which query — the semantic ladder, grain rules, perspectives themselves.

### L8 — Semantic zoom and the ladder `[Sid→ratified]`
Altitude is not scale; it is a different projection (Sid's Google Earth: buildings → city → state). Each space's ladder — what ten-thousand-feet *means* here — is authored material, per space, per perspective; Google hard-codes theirs, which is why Earth is "set." The ladder is populated by named boundaries (L10): altitude shows names instead of members.

### L9 — Clocks and condensation `[Sid→ratified]`
Three clocks: **frame** (local, ~16ms: camera, hover, gesture in flight), **presence** (Electric: cursors, viewports, the living — flows to peers, never lands in the record), **record** (Rama: what durably happened). Law: **nothing crosses to a slower clock except through a condensation policy.** Persistence is a condensation some streams earn (Sid: don't store sixty pans a second in Rama), and condensation rules are themselves facts. Transports are kernel (physics of frame/network/disk).

### L10 — Boundaries: reification of groups `[Sid→ratified]`
The move: take a plurality, draw a boundary, name it — the name becomes an object carrying law and meaning. **Law flows down** (theme → members), **meaning flows up** (fifty notes → the hypothesis space they point to). Boundaries are overlays, never containment: many per thing, perspectival ("arbitrary depending on who the user, the group, the agent is" — Sid), non-interfering. Containment stays the single kernel tree (coordinate frames, layout, hit-testing, cascade); one tree for space, unlimited overlays for meaning. Two kinds that age differently: **drawn** (hand-picked, tended) and **defined** (a query that got a name; updates itself). Lens vs testimony is a provenance-kind: counted vs claimed-by-this-mind-over-these-members.

### L11 — The fractal treaty: userland waists `[Sid→ratified]`
The kernel/userland split recurses: protocol stewards (e.g. discourse graphs — Sid's example) define types, relations, validators — grammar with a floor — and instance-makers live inside it without seeing its mechanics ("a product user that should not know how Softland is" — Sid). Sid→kernel :: steward→schema :: group→space-policies :: anyone→instances. Each level a complete world for its inhabitants; each held by an arbitrary combination of humans, agents, groups. Consequence: **a software is an import, not a development** — adopting a protocol = importing a bundle of material (type-facts, face-bindings, default policies, a ladder entry). The discourse-graph app is the designated first proof: a whole product, zero lines below the waist.

### L12 — Compilers from material `[ratified]`
Above-the-waist does not mean slow: the kernel's most leveraged citizens are compilers that take material (an assembly, constraint set, standing query, ladder) and compile it into the hot form. Precedents already in the stack: Electric compiles a program into client/server dataflow; Rama compiles topology descriptions; GPUs compile shader text at runtime. This is the domesticated form of the eval door (Clojure: code is data), which is why the kernel can shrink over time instead of only being chosen once.

### L13 — Composition, split `[Sid→ratified]`
"Composition law" bundles two strata (Sid's Figma catch): the **mechanism** of containment (kernel: single, universal — frames inherit, transforms compose, layout walks, hit-test walks back, deletion cascades) and the **policies** of when composition happens and what gestures mean (facts, per space: canvas says overlap⇒child; graph says proximity⇒suggested edge; 3D says insideness⇒volumetric attachment). "Where is that defined / what if I redefine it" — in a policy-fact; edit the fact. Caveat at the seam: layout constraint *kinds* need solver support, so a new constraint kind is a legitimate fence-trip.

### L14 — The substrate boundary `[ratified]`
Everything above covers the material and its machinery. It does not cover adjudication between perspectives: whose boundary wins where they overlap, whether two protocols can claim one instance, when a changed group's name still speaks, whose cascade-order resolves a collision. The material can hold anyone's law; it cannot say whose law holds. That is the inhabitants' territory — Sid's "economy," held behind a door he opens when ready (an MD file exists; unread as of this writing).
