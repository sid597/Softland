# 3D — the starter (session 0, 2026-09-05)

Essence of Sid's 3D exploration in clean first person (his words of 2026-08-10, 2026-08-26, 2026-09-04 and today, not quoted); frames he adopted are stated as a working basis to attack; none of any session's answers as conclusions. Three complete versions per the optimise-prompt skill. Send **Irrespective** to both lanes, identical: the path round showed each rider alone pulls one strength. "3D facets" is session speak and is not used.

## Fable

`````
I am working through the engine under `src/app/client/` one kind at a time, at the level of inputs and outputs: what goes into a kind, what comes out, and whether the kind is really a library. The path kind, ink and shapes, has been worked to a picture; it lives in `docs/below-the-waist/path-kind/`, and `path-kind.md` with section 3 of `HANDOFF-8.md` say where it stands. Now the 3D kind, and this session is for it.

What I am trying to build is every tool, 2D and 3D. The way I want to get there is a waist: an engine and a few hardcoded primitives below it, and everything above defined as data, ECS-style, that humans and agents create, edit, save for reuse and build higher things from, without merge deadlocks. The hardest 3D tools are the ceilings: parametric CAD, procedural film tools, real-time open worlds, BIM, planet-scale geospatial, volumes and scans; `docs/below-the-waist/3d-ceilings-starter.md` names them. I do not want to hack through the ceilings, I want to build through them. So the question is what below-the-waist layer should exist for everything, 2D and 3D in one answer, or a reason there are two.

Where the exploration stands. Today Region3D is a 3D region embedded in the 2D world: a whole scene as declared data with its own camera, depth, lights and picking, rendered offscreen and composited into the page as one ordered rectangle, and text and ink can be placed on planes inside it. I have asked before whether that is the right long-term containment, 3D living in 2D, 2D living in 3D, or a more general model of nested planar and spatial spaces, and I want it judged from what Softland is for, not from the shape of the current renderer. I want the 3D theory first: what a 3D thing is and what it is made of. I want one model for 2D and 3D in one window that is elegant and plays with differential reactive data and mouse actions over data, so reactivity can be fine-grained; click in, "you hit this" out; and hover matters, because hover is where a person's focus is. Now the question is how 3D gets built out and whether it composes with the path picture.

Working basis I have adopted and want attacked rather than assumed. In the path round, every session that started from rendering made a definitional error, and every session that started from what the object must mean made none. In the field, a 3D scene is objects with transforms, geometry, materials, lights and a camera, and rendering is projection, visibility and shading; the interchange forms are settled and the execution is contested, the same split the 2D imaging model has. One composition primitive joins the two models: render a scene of either into a surface, and a surface is paint in the other, in both directions and recursively. The 2D contract ends at a region drawn with per-pixel coverage; whether 3D begins there is open.

Questions I am carrying:
- What is the general 3D object, and what are its sources? A modelled mesh, a formula solid, a scan or a splat cloud, a 2D shape given depth, a whole scene. Is the region the thing, or one container of things?
- Containment: 3D inside 2D as today, 2D inside 3D, or nested spaces of both kinds. What must a 3D thing continue to mean after I orbit it, move an object, place ink on a face, zoom the page it sits in, or share it?
- Two cameras: the page's zoom and the region's camera. What composes them, and what happens when the page's zoom crosses into a region?
- Picking and hover through the seam: click in, "you hit this" out, when the thing hit is ink on a face of an object inside a region on a page.
- Four things 2D never had: a mark's pixels depend on other marks (shadows, occlusion, transparency order); many shape representations at once, where converting between them is the work; 2D and 3D nest both ways, including 2D derived from 3D (sections, silhouettes); ranges and budgets past float32 and per-frame time. Which of these force code below the waist, and which are data above it?
- What must exist as code below the waist for 3D, and what is its input and output, such that the ceilings can be built above it as data?
- Am I reasoning from what region3d does today or from how it should be? If the best team in the field were handed this, how would they define the problem, and what trade-offs would they name that I do not know to ask about?

How to get at the code: use the hierarchical docs inside `src/app/client/` to reach any level you need. Start at `src/app/client/README.md`, then the folder's README, then namespace and function docstrings, then the code. Docs allowed: `docs/below-the-waist/path-kind/` and `docs/below-the-waist/3d-ceilings-starter.md`, nothing else. Never `src/app/server/env.clj`. `docs/below-the-waist/3d/` is this exploration's directory. This is exploration, the phase before any contract. I want to end up with a mental model I can hold, and a clear picture of what must exist as code for 3D, its input and output, and why, so that every session on this is talking about the same thing.

Where you take a position, on containment, on the general 3D object, on one waist or two: what supports it, under what conditions does it become only a workable default rather than the answer, and what else in the picture changes with it, including in the path-kind picture?
`````
