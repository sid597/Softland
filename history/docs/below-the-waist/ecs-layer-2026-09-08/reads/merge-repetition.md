**The expectation broke as a prediction that Codex would leave three separate things.** Both readers found a common thing across the vocabularies. The difference is how much each makes follow from that identification: Claude assembles a mount and one execution loop from the builds' complementary parts; Codex identifies a representation instance and separately names the relationships and machinery supporting it. Claude's more ambitious consolidation held; the predicted split between the readers did not.

This compares the two reads, without answering the original layer question or selecting either proposal. Its ground is only the six supplied documents. References below are document line numbers, not inspected code.

**Agreement, once.** The recurring addition connects a subject, a tool and a situated view around inherited execution and rendering. It carries identity into inspection and editing, preserves working context, and needs a browser host. Much composition and interaction still lives in client code. Both readers see executable records for those relationships as a way to shrink the next build. “mounted tool” and “representation instance” substantially share that center.

The first row is a difference in packaging. Codex's five pieces are five recurring responsibilities, not three unrelated builds. The scheduler row is the sharpest difference in what the reader thinks can already be derived. Claude also explicitly says no build made the scheduler generic (C:27); its stronger claim is that combining the documented ingredients makes the generic loop follow. Codex leaves that design unsettled.

The shared documents support the distinction Codex draws there: Place describes both complete assembled-input comparison and specific client dispatch (E:183–201); Workspace explicitly lacks an automatic dependency graph (W:86). They do not demonstrate the proposed combined loop. Likewise, Workspace distinguishes a placement name from the whole layout (W:80), Place says moving UI behavior into records needs vocabulary and execution support (E:342), and Vantage leaves general window composition unimplemented (V:258). Those statements establish what remains; they do not establish that Claude's proposed placement field settles it.

The editing row is an additional extraction by Codex, not a direct contradiction: compiled gestures can coexist with records specifying field targets and commit policy. Claude does not name that independent binding in its proposed mount. The selector row is also a difference of scope rather than a rejection: Codex later asks for shared reference semantics (D:71), but does not build Vantage's selected authoring layers into its initial common definition.

**The expectation, tested against this pair.**

> claude sees sameness across the three vocabularies; codex reports three things unless a write-up itself says they are one. where codex agrees, the sameness is in the docs, not in the reader.

- **“claude sees sameness across the three vocabularies” held.** Claude explicitly calls its object “the union at the seam, not one build's shape” (C:41). Its proposal combines capabilities that its own comparison says no single build possesses. That is stronger than matching names.
- **“codex reports three things unless a write-up itself says they are one” broke.** Codex's central statement is: “All three built that relationship. They distributed it differently across records and application code.” (D:13.) Its table crosses all three vocabularies under shared pieces (D:21–27). No supplied write-up declares that Place's distributed declarations and projections, Workspace's representation record, and Vantage's tool/page assembly are one cross-build object. That identification is Codex's synthesis.
- **“where codex agrees, the sameness is in the docs, not in the reader” holds only in the weaker sense that the descriptions make the connection available.** Workspace gives particularly direct language: “Independent subject/tool references, view choice and a placement such as main, companion or kept.” (W:80.) But identifying the other builds with that relationship still requires a reader. Codex also derives a shared editing record with conversion and commit policy from differently implemented edits (E:259–263; W:58; V:104–109); none of those accounts supplies that unified record. Its contribution is more than recognizing an equivalence already stated for it.

What survived is a difference in the reach of the consolidation. Claude recruits complementary parts into one proposed mechanism and predicts that several application rules disappear. Codex makes its own cross-build abstractions while explicitly leaving some unshared semantics outside the common result. This pair supports that distinction; it does not support a general rule that one reader sees sameness and the other cannot.

The silences therefore overlap most closely on reference resolution and Workspace's command-driven execution, and partially on composition. Claude inventories more missing representation fields, execution details and compatibility between builds. Codex's distinctive silence is whether a correspondence can be discovered or continue to mean the same thing after the subject changes. Neither inventory contains the other.

Two qualifications keep that accounting honest. Claude's numbered item 12, results as records, says explicitly: “This one is stated, not missing, and the three diverge” (C:104). It is not a twelfth documentary gap: the accounts describe in-memory world results, durable Workspace results and temporary Vantage browser results (E:218–219; W:68, 76; V:261). Codex's statements that persistence and authoring layers are not common to all builds (D:10, 61, 71), and that new editing semantics still need implementation (D:59), likewise mark known scope boundaries rather than missing descriptions.

**Where the named silences do not restrain the conclusion.** This matters to the expectation: counting caveats is not the same as seeing what a reader allows those caveats to limit.

- Claude's comparison marks Vantage's address-bound roots **“No”** (C:34), then says the account never says whether a root can name another record (C:93). The four documents warrant the latter uncertainty; absence of a described binding is not proof that such a binding is absent. The proposed union is stated more conclusively than this ingredient is established.
- Claude says one repaired adapter “replaces all three” (C:83), then explicitly leaves Workspace's compatibility with that route unknown (C:98). The replacement is a proposed result, not a consequence already demonstrated by the accounts. Codex does not take up that consolidation, so there is no opposing Codex verdict to quote.
- Claude says of the vantage that “its parent chain is the piece all three kept as data” (C:87). Place and Vantage describe navigation predecessors (E:272–275; V:81–89). Workspace describes a parent **revision** in its entity envelope and saved working context (W:64, 81), without describing the same navigation chain. Claude does not flag that distinction. Codex's working-context claim does not require all three to have that chain (D:65–71).
- Claude's Vantage run-record question starts from “a run is addressable behind a hit” (C:100, pointing to V:53). V:50–59 is about text and uses “source/tool/line/run”; that wording does not itself establish an executor-run identity. The record question can remain open, but the document does not supply the stronger premise. Codex does not make it.

Codex's unresolved composition language, reference rule and scheduler design remain unresolved in its proposal. It nevertheless proposes records spanning those boundaries; calling them candidates does not make the missing semantics available. Its restraint here is that it identifies those semantics as further work. Claude often names the missing join with greater specificity, while still writing as though the proposed union will remove the surrounding rules. That is the consequential difference this pair exposes.
